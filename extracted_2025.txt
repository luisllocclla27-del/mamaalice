Health  
General Objective 
Children and families at social risk receive psychosocial and health support through 
promotion, prevention, and intervention activities in order to improve their quality of life 
during 2025. 
Key Achievements 
1. Immediate Attention for Special Cases 
During 2025, six special medical cases were identified and attended immediately. 
Beneficiaries received accompaniment, referrals, and follow-up care through different health 
institutions. Cases included suspected pregnancy, fractures, neurological problems, dental 
complications, skin conditions, and suspected sexually transmitted infections. All cases 
received complete medical attention through public insurance (SIS) or private care, achieving 
a 100% response rate. 
2. Healthy Family Program 
Seven beneficiary families were identified with deficiencies in hygiene and household 
organization. Five families actively participated in workshops focused on home cleanliness, 
personal and oral hygiene, and shared household responsibilities. Two families could not fully 
participate. One of them due to limited availability and the other family due to lack of 
interest. Positive changes were observed in participating households, including cleaner living 
environments and improved waste management practices. 
3. First Aid and Emergency Response 
A total of 75 first aid cases were attended during 2025, including injuries from falls, 
headaches, fever, stomach pain, dental pain, fungal infections, nosebleeds, sore throats, and 
dog bites. Most injuries occurred among younger children during recreational activities. 
Increased cases of fungal infections were observed during November and December. 
4. Oral Health Program 
A total of 110 children were enrolled in the oral health program, and 107 actively 
participated in preventive and treatment activities. 
• 97 children participated in supervised brushing and oral hygiene education sessions. 
• 10 children received dental treatment for advanced cavities in coordination with a 
partner dental clinic and with parental consent. 
Dental evaluations showed that many children required extensive treatment, averaging 
between 9 and 11 affected teeth per child. Eight children completed their dental treatment 
successfully, while two discontinued due to fear caused by previous dental experiences. 

--- PAGE 2 ---
The program contributed to stronger oral hygiene habits and greater awareness of 
preventive care. 
5. Sexual Education Program 
Twenty-eight sixth-grade students enrolled in the sexual education program, and 22 
completed the workshops successfully. Four educational sessions addressed Human 
development stages, Puberty and adolescence, Physical and emotional changes, 
Menstruation and reproductive organs and Personal hygiene during adolescence.  
At the beginning, many participants showed fear and embarrassment discussing these topics, 
but over time they became more confident and engaged. Parents also expressed 
appreciation for the workshops, noting the difficulty of discussing these topics at home. 
Additional Youth Workshops 
Special sexual education workshops were also organized for young people aged 17–23 
participating in the vocational training program. Topics included reproductive health, sexually 
transmitted infections (STIs), and contraceptive methods. The workshops promoted informed 
and responsible decision-making regarding sexual and reproductive health. In general 
youngsters are very interested and are happy with our open communication about these 
topics.  
Monitoring and Impact 
Results were monitored continuously through weekly observations, attendance records, and 
evaluations before and after educational sessions. Improvements were observed in children’s 
oral hygiene, personal hygiene, self-confidence, and healthy decision-making. 
Collaboration with Institutions 
The program worked in coordination with several institutions, including: 
• KARIDENT Dental Clinic 
• San Miguel Arcángel Pediatric Clinic 
• Regional Hospital of Ayacucho 
• Hospital de la Solidaridad 
• Vista Alegre Health Center 
• Yanama Health Post 
These partnerships strengthened trust among families and expanded access to specialized 
healthcare services. 
Achievements and Positive Outcomes

The health team observed significant progress throughout the year. Children increasingly 
approached the health area independently for oral hygiene support, and participating 
families demonstrated continuous improvements in household cleanliness and healthy living 
practices. Educational materials and playful learning methods were especially effective in 
encouraging healthy habits among children. 
Areas for Improvement 
The team identified the following areas for further development: 
• Strengthening decision-making processes 
• Improving annual planning and organization 
• Expanding educational workshops for different target groups 
• Creating separate groups within the sexual education program according to age and 
needs 
Future Plans 
Key priorities for the coming year include: 
• Expanding health education workshops 
• Strengthening partnerships with clinics, hospitals, and professional organizations 
• Increasing preventive health activities 
• Developing formal cooperation agreements with medical institutions and professional 
associations 
The organization recognizes the importance of continued collaboration with external 
institutions to improve healthcare access and strengthen family trust in the program.

Annual Social Area Report – 2025 Summary 
General Objective – Wellbeing Program 
NARS and families at social risk received support through a bio-psycho-social approach 
focused on promotion, prevention, and intervention in order to improve their quality of life 
during 2025. 
This objective was achieved through coordinated work between the social, psychology, and 
health areas, providing comprehensive support to children and families. Activities included 
individual counseling, emotional support, family interventions, mental health promotion, and 
safe spaces where children and families could freely express and manage their emotions. 
Creative and recreational activities such as games, crafts, painting, and socio-emotional 
workshops were used to strengthen imagination, teamwork, frustration tolerance, emotional 
regulation, communication, and healthy coexistence. The “Rock and Water” workshops 
further supported body awareness, self-control, confidence, and peaceful conflict resolution. 
Together, these activities contributed significantly to the emotional and social wellbeing of 
participating children and families. 
Family Festival 
The Family Festival strengthened emotional bonds between children and their families by 
creating moments of joy, recreation, and healthy interaction. Parents and children actively 
participated in activities that promoted communication, unity, and mutual respect. 
One challenge was partial attendance due to work obligations, as well as difficulties with 
punctuality among some families. 
Counseling and Guidance 
During 2025, counseling and guidance services were successfully provided to families and 
NARS according to their specific needs. 
• The social area attended 12 families. 
• The psychology area accompanied 11 families. 
• The health area supported 10 families. 
Many women approached the social area primarily seeking a safe space to be heard and 
emotionally supported. In several cases, emotional containment and orientation alone 
helped them feel calmer and more capable of addressing their situations independently. 
Adolescents also voluntarily sought support related to relationships, emotional confusion, 
heartbreak, and peer conflicts. Through mediation and dialogue, many learned to resolve 
conflicts respectfully, apologize, and strengthen emotional awareness.

Emotional Self-Care and Creative Activities

appropriate to their developmental stage. 
• Harmful misconceptions related to relationships and sexuality were clarified. 
The intervention contributed to healthier interactions and stronger emotional skills among 
participants.

GASTRONOMY PROGRAM – 2025 
Annual Summary Report 
General Objective 
The Gastronomy Program aims to help young people learn a professional trade while 
strengthening their social behavior, personal development, and future opportunities for 
employment and self-improvement. 
The program combines intensive culinary training with personal guidance, emotional 
support, and real-life work experience. Throughout the year, students not only learned how 
to work in a professional kitchen, but also developed resilience, confidence, responsibility, 
and the ability to overcome personal challenges. 
 
Specific Objectives – 2025 
1. Strengthen social behavior and personal development 
Throughout the year, students participated in workshops focused on emotional 
development, communication, teamwork, self-esteem, conflict resolution, and healthy 
boundaries. The “Rock and Water” program also helped students improve self-awareness, 
emotional regulation, and confidence. 
The first months were challenging. Many students struggled with homesickness, insecurity, 
and adapting to communal living. Some difficulties included disrespectful behavior, breaking 
dormitory rules, hidden alcohol consumption, and conflicts between students. However, 
through consistent guidance, emotional support, and patient accompaniment, significant 
growth became visible in most students. 
Several students who initially arrived withdrawn, insecure, or emotionally distant gradually 
became more confident, expressive, and socially connected. Others learned to take 
responsibility for their actions, reflect on their choices, and develop healthier relationships 
with peers and authority figures. 
One of the most emotional achievements of the year was seeing students who once doubted 
themselves begin to believe in their own abilities. Some students who barely spoke at the 
beginning eventually presented final projects with confidence, led group activities, or 
successfully completed internships in demanding professional kitchens. 
Although not every challenge was fully resolved, important progress was achieved in areas 
such as: 
• Self-esteem and confidence 
• Emotional expression 

--- PAGE 22 ---
• Respectful coexistence 
• Teamwork and communication 
• Responsibility and resilience 
• Setting healthy boundaries 
• Leadership and initiative 
 
2. Develop professional culinary skills 
Most students successfully acquired the technical skills expected from an entry-level cook. 
Through continuous practice, they learned: 
• Knife skills and food handling 
• Mise en place organization 
• Cooking techniques 
• Team coordination during service 
• Hygiene and food safety 
• Working under pressure 
• Plating and presentation 
Students showed different learning rhythms and strengths. Some excelled technically and 
worked quickly, while others stood out for organization, discipline, creativity, or teamwork. 
Because training was highly practical from the first weeks onward, students gained real 
experience early in the program. The restaurant “QuinuaQ” became a key training 
environment where students practiced in real service conditions and learned how to manage 
stress, timing, and customer interaction. 
Several students demonstrated remarkable personal growth alongside technical 
improvement. Students who initially feared making mistakes gradually became capable of 
handling professional responsibilities in real restaurants. 
Chefs from internship restaurants consistently highlighted the strong preparation, discipline, 
and adaptability of the students compared to trainees from other institutions. 
At the end of the program: 
• 18 students successfully completed the full training process. 
• 12 graduates quickly found employment in restaurants. 
• Multiple students received direct job offers from their internship locations.

Psychological Support and Family Well-being 
In 2025, Mama Alice continued to provide psychological support to children, adolescents 
and families facing difficult social and family circumstances. The Psychology Department 
combined individual therapy and counselling with preventive activities, creative 
programmes and support for parents. The aim was not only to respond when problems 
arose, but also to strengthen emotional resilience, family relationships and the skills 
children and parents need in everyday life. 
Individual psychological support 
During the year, 14 children, adolescents and adults received individual psychological 
support. The cases referred to the psychologist were often complex and included anxiety, 
depression, behavioural difficulties, family conflict, domestic violence, substance abuse, 
learning difficulties and experiences of abuse. Some required urgent and intensive 
intervention. 
Despite the complexity of these situations, important progress was made. Eleven clients 
were able to complete their therapeutic process after showing clear improvements in their 
emotional and psychological well-being. Improvements included better emotional 
regulation, fewer behavioural problems, greater self-confidence and better functioning at 
home and at school. Two cases required longer-term support and continued into the 
following period. 
In addition to therapy, 11 children, adolescents and parents received psychological 
counselling. Parents most frequently sought advice about raising their children, setting 
boundaries, changes during adolescence and dealing with separation within the family. 
Adolescents also approached the psychologist voluntarily to talk about friendships, 
relationships, conflicts with peers and difficulties at home. 
One of the challenges remained involving parents consistently in the therapeutic process. 
Work obligations and difficult economic circumstances often make regular attendance 
difficult. Nevertheless, many mothers in particular became actively involved and practised 
at home what they had learned during counselling and therapy. 
A safe place to grow 
Psychological well-being is supported not only through therapy. Every week, more than 30 
children and adolescents participated in recreational, cognitive and creative activities 
organised by the Psychology Department. 
Board games, puzzles and group activities helped strengthen attention, memory and 
concentration, while drawing, painting, dancing, outdoor activities and handicrafts 
provided opportunities to relax, express emotions and develop creativity. Just as 
importantly, children practised sharing, respecting others, following rules, making their 
own choices and solving problems independently. 

--- PAGE 31 ---
The changes became increasingly visible during the year. At first, many children quickly 
said that activities were “too difficult” and continuously asked adults for help. Others 
struggled with sharing materials, waiting their turn or respecting each other's work. 
Gradually, they became more independent and confident and took responsibility for 
cleaning up and caring for the materials themselves. 
A particularly touching example occurred when a new girl became frustrated during a 
handicraft activity because her work did not turn out as she had hoped. Before an adult 
needed to intervene, other children gathered around her and reassured her: mistakes were 
normal, she was still learning, and what mattered was that she had made it herself. Their 
spontaneous reaction showed that lessons about patience, respect and self-worth had 
become part of the way they treated one another. 
Working with mothers and families 
Supporting a child also means supporting the family around them. During 2025, Mama 
Alice therefore continued its workshops for mothers on positive parenting and emotional 
self-care. 
Topics included respectful parenting, managing emotions at home, setting healthy 
boundaries, children's use of screens and time, stress management and self-care. The 
workshops were highly practical and interactive. Mothers were encouraged to share 
experiences, questions and concerns in an environment where they felt safe and 
supported. 
Of the 16 mothers invited, eight attended every workshop throughout the programme. For 
others, regular attendance was difficult, particularly because many work informally, travel 
to rural areas for work or face economic pressures that make it impossible to attend every 
meeting. 
The impact of the workshops was also visible at home. Children told us that their families 
were using practical tools introduced during the sessions to organise screen time and talk 
about emotions. One child proudly explained that she and her mother used the “emotional 
traffic light” every day. For some mothers, the workshops were also a rare opportunity to 
focus on their own well-being. After a relaxation exercise, several mothers commented 
that they never had activities like this for themselves and that they had really needed it. 
Family Festival 
At the end of the year, the Psychology, Social Work and Health Departments jointly 
organised the Family Festival. Ten families were invited in recognition of their commitment 
and the progress they had made during the year. 
Through games and family activities, parents and children demonstrated what they had 
learned about emotions, communication, respectful parenting and living together. The 
participating families included children who had initially struggled with anxiety, 

--- PAGE 32 ---
behavioural or learning difficulties, or difficult family situations. By the end of the year, 
improvements could be seen not only in the children, but also in the way family members 
communicated and interacted with one another. 
For us, this is an important measure of success. Psychological support should not remain 
inside the consultation room. Its real value becomes visible when children feel safer, 
parents respond differently, communication improves and families develop tools they can 
continue to use independently. 
Looking ahead 
In the coming year, Mama Alice aims to continue and strengthen psychological support for 
vulnerable children, adolescents and families. An important priority will be practical 
workshops for parents and children, providing tools that families can continue to use even 
after Mama Alice is no longer working directly in their community. 
The Psychology Department also plans to provide training for the Mama Alice team, 
helping staff strengthen their knowledge and their ability to support beneficiaries in 
different contexts. In addition, greater attention will be given to staff well-being through 
workshops on self-care, stress management, communication and teamwork. 
Ultimately, the aim is not simply to solve individual problems, but to help children and 
families become stronger, more confident and better equipped to face future challenges 
independently.
