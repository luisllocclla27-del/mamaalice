Dear Mama Alice Friends, 
Compared to previous years, marked by the severe impacts of COVID-19
in Peru and ongoing political unrest, 2024 has been a peaceful and
stable year in Ayacucho. This allowed us to develop numerous activities
for the children and their families at Mama Alice, focused on their well-
being and growth. It was also a significant year, as, with the support of
Stichting Wilde Ganzen, several Dutch funds, and many friends, we
embarked on the construction of bedrooms for students from rural areas.
In 2025, we will begin a new chapter by launching an internal vocational
training program for young people in bartending, waitering, and
cooking. The students will stay with us for 3.5 weeks, followed by a half-
week break. 
Additionally, with the help of the UEFA Foundation for Children, we laid
out a small grass field where the children of Mama Alice can practice
football, and where we also conduct Rock and Water training sessions.
This year has been a testament to progress, hope, and new
opportunities, and we are grateful for the trust and support that make
our work possible.
I want to thank you on behalf of all our children and the Mama Alice
team for your wonderful support in 2024. Only together can we make this
small corner of the world a brighter and more beautiful place for
hundreds of children, young people, and their families.
Frederique Kallen 
Founder NGO Mama Alice

Ayacucho was once the center of the pre-Hispanic Huari culture, and it was
the Battle of Ayacucho that sealed Latin American independence. In
recent history, however, Ayacucho has been marked by
underdevelopment, isolation and poverty. People from the rural areas
migrate to the city of Ayacucho, hoping to make a better life for
themselves. These economical refugees settle on the outskirts of Ayacucho,
where they lack basic facilities such as water, sanitation and electricity,
which in turn leads to an increase in infectious diseases. Unemployment is
high and can result in poverty, family break-ups and violence, malnutrition
or even the abandoning of children. The economic development of Peru is
noticeable in Ayacucho, but the poorest communities hardly participate in
this progress. Our activities remain very necessary here.

Economic growth
1.In 2024, one of the most notable developments in the
Mining Sector: The mining industry continued to be a major
driver of Peru’s economy in 2024. Major investments were
made in new mining projects and the expansion of
existing ones, particularly in copper and gold. This
contributed to increased exports and helped boost
national income.
2.Tourism Resurgence: After the disruptions caused by the
pandemic, the tourism sector saw a significant recovery in
2024. New marketing campaigns and improved
infrastructure attracted more international visitors,
especially to iconic sites like Machu Picchu and Cusco,
providing a vital boost to local economies and creating
jobs.
 Protests
Overall, 2024 was marked by both social activism and 
notable economic developments.
In 2024, Peru witnessed a large-scale protest movement
that gained significant momentum. The protests primarily
took place in the capital, Lima, as well as in other major
cities such as Arequipa and Trujillo, around mid-year. The
demonstrators were calling for government reforms,
including measures to address corruption, improve social
justice, and reduce economic inequality.
The protests attracted tens of thousands of participants, with
estimates often ranging from 50,000 to over 100,000 people at
their peak. The movement was largely peaceful but
experienced some tensions with authorities. This wave of
activism underscored widespread public dissatisfaction and
was seen as a pivotal moment for social and political change
in Peru.
POLITICAL SITUATIONPERU 2024
In 2024, Peru's political landscape remains
marked by instability and ongoing
struggles to establish lasting stability. The
country has experienced frequent
changes in government, with recent
presidencies facing significant challenges
related to corruption, governance, and
public dissatisfaction. Political polarization
is still prominent, often leading to protests
and civil unrest, as citizens demand
reforms, transparency, and better
economic policies.
The legislative branch continues to play a
crucial role, often clashing with the
executive over policy directions. Efforts
towards constitutional reform and anti-
corruption measures have been central
topics on the political agenda. The
judiciary and anti-corruption institutions
are actively involved in investigating high-
profile cases, which has both bolstered
public trust in some institutions and fueled
political conflicts.
In the electoral sphere, recent elections
have seen increased participation and a
push for more reformist and social justice-
oriented candidates. The government is
also focusing on addressing social issues
such as inequality, indigenous rights, and
environmental concerns, particularly
related to mining and other natural
resource industries.
Overall, while there are signs of political
convergence around reformist agendas,
Peru faces ongoing challenges in
maintaining political stability, combating
corruption, and implementing effective
governance. The country's future
trajectory depends on successful
reconciliation between different political
factions and continued efforts toward
democratization and social inclusion.

International assessments 
The creation of the Programme for International Student Assessment
(PISA) in 2000 outlined a common policy agenda to assess
educational quality and foster evidence-based policies. The PISA
assessment tests 15-year-old students in at least seventh grade and
measures not just whether students can reproduce what they have
learned, but also how well students can apply their knowledge in
unfamiliar settings, inside and outside of school. 
PISA assesses knowledge and skills in mathematics, science, and
reading, and is conducted every three years. However, the eighth
round, scheduled for 2021, was postponed to 2022 due to the
COVID-19 pandemic. Forty-three countries participated in the first
round of PISA (2000), including five Latin American countries:
Argentina, Brazil, Chile, Mexico and Peru.

EDUCATION 
General Structure: Peru's education system is divided into several levels: initial
(pre-primary), primary, secondary, and higher education. Despite progress,
the system faces several infrastructural, quality, and access challenges.
Key Points in 2024:
Coverage and Progress:
 In recent years, Peru has made significant strides in expanding early
childhood education and primary education coverage. However,
challenges remain in secondary education, with drop-out rates and
disparities in access, especially in rural areas.
Infrastructure Issues:
 About 60% of public schools lack basic facilities such as clean water,
electricity, and sanitation. Many institutions require structural
reinforcement or complete renovation to meet minimum standards.
Institutional Conditions:

HISTORY 
Twelve years ago, Frederique was asked by the then
board of Mama Alice Netherlands to look for a
source of income in Peru itself. In Ayacucho, we
explored the possibilities of starting an (export)
business selling Mamitas handicraft products, setting
up a (training) restaurant and selling photo cards of
our children and surroundings. We also considered
starting a tour operator and a number of other
initiatives.
After consulting with the board and team of NGO
Mama Alice and companies with experience in
Ayacucho, we concluded that a hotel with a
restaurant would be the best option for us. Especially
since we could combine it with vocational training to
become waiters, bartenders, cooks and
chambermaids.
With great enthusiasm from friends, family and
businesses, we received sufficient funds to submit an
application to the ´Wilde Ganzen Foundation´ for the
construction of a training restaurant. We had to put
the idea of building a hotel on hold for a while, as the
budget was insufficient.
We purchased a beautiful piece of land with a view
of the foothills of the Andes Mountains. We
encountered quite a few problems during
construction, but we always found a solution. From
the beginning, many Ayacuchans come on
weekends to enjoy the delicious food and fresh air.

An important core value of NGO Mama Alice is the implementation of a transparent financial policy.
NGO Mama Alice has laid down the guidelines for a transparent financial statement in an
'Administration Handbook'. The NGO receives donations from national and international donors.
Because most donors and providers of funds from Mama Alice don't live in Ayacucho, it is extra
important to show transparently how the donations are spent. Since the founding of Mama Alice Peru
in 2005, we have built trust with donors and funds through our transparent and professional financial
policy. This financial report is a short version, in English, of the complete annual report of NGO Mama
Alice. Just like in previous years, our annual statement is audited by an independent accountant.
NGO Mama Alice has an internet bank account in Peru. A 'view only' option has been chosen at the
bank, which means that nobody can 'move' money with internet banking. Salaries and large
payments are paid using cheques. The cheques are issued by the bank at our request. Flor Cuenca,
our accountant, assigns and controls the cheques using the accounting system ‘Contable’. Every
cheque is checked and, after approval, signed by director Elba Zegarra and coordinator Zoila
Rosales. Flor then hands out the cheque and checks the payment invoices and receipts of, for
example, water and electricity. The expenditure of NGO Mama Alice is only made within the
framework of the budget. When there is a need for additional expenses, this is only done with the
permission of the donor, the fund or partner. 
In 2024, social worker Zoila (the second person to sign the cheques) was replaced by teacher Eskarly
for two reasons:
1.Every five years, the second person to sign the cheques is replaced.
2.Zoila previously worked part-time as coordinator of the vocational training programme and was
therefore often present at the office. Zoila now works full-time in Yanama and is therefore more
difficult to reach. Eskarly is coordinator of the education group and is regularly present at the
office, which simplifies the coordination of signing cheques.
NGO Mama Alice is registered with APCI (Agencia Peruana de Cooperación Internacional), a
government agency that monitors international donations. Every month the accounting is digitally sent
to SUNAT (superintendencia Nacional de Administracion Tributaria), the National Superintendency of
Taks Administration. SUNAT has audited Mama Alice each year and Mama Alice passed the checks
without any difficulty. With this we can furthermore guarantee the good performance and transparent
work of Mama Alice. APCI, according to its control action plan, has supervised the 2024 period, both
financial and work in the fields, having a favorable result. Every month the PDT 621 - 601 (Telematic
Declaration Program) is sent to SUNAT (National Superintendence of Tax Administration) in compliance
with tax regulations. Also the books and electronic records are sent monthly.
